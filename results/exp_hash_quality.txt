
=== Experiment 3: hash quality on realistic integer keys ========
identity_hash is exactly what libstdc++ and libc++ do for integers.
Combined with a power-of-two mask, it is the table's entire defence
against structured input -- which is to say, none.

          hash         keys   insert ns    hit ns   miss ns   grp/hit     p99   cmp/hit  
--------------  -----------  ----------  --------  --------  --------  ------  --------  
      identity       random       91.04     84.57     29.14     1.000       1     1.004  
        fmix64       random       52.63    108.83     31.59     1.000       1     1.004  
        wyhash       random       60.16    116.86     34.03     1.000       1     1.004  
      identity   sequential      865.50    855.78   1054.60   175.787      32    29.708  
        fmix64   sequential       25.16     50.81     15.83     1.000       1     1.004  
        wyhash   sequential       28.19     45.28     14.14     1.019       2     1.010  
      identity      strided       67.06    167.36    192.94     1.000       1     8.500  
        fmix64      strided       25.05     50.90     15.71     1.000       1     1.004  
        wyhash      strided       27.84     44.83     14.24     1.001       1     1.003  

The two identity failures are different failures, and only the counters tell
them apart:

  sequential  H1 = hash >> 7, so 128 consecutive integers share a home group.
              The group is 16 slots wide. The table is 8x oversubscribed at every
              home position and the probe walks a huge cluster: watch grp/hit.
              This refutes the hypothesis at the top of this file, which assumed
              the table indexes by the hash rather than by H1.

  strided     H2 = hash & 0x7F, and i << 12 has no low bits, so every key stores
              fingerprint 0. Home groups are still well spread (grp/hit stays at
              1.0), but the filter matches every occupied slot in the group, so
              each lookup pays a full key comparison per resident: watch cmp/hit.
              This is the H2 ablation of Experiment 4, arrived at by accident
              through the key distribution rather than on purpose through config.

Neither is repairable by better probing. This is the argument for the table owning
its mixer rather than inheriting whatever std::hash the key type came with.

Timing noise on this run: worst (max-min)/min across all measurements was 337.4%.
That is high. Timings below are best-of-7 and still carry that much run-to-run
variation, so read the counter columns as the result and the nanoseconds as
corroboration. See RESULTS.md for this machine's configuration.
